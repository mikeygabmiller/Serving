# Serving the Traditional Latin Mass (1962 Missale Romanum): research findings

Researched 2026-10-05 for a server-training website. Scope: the Mass of the 1962 Missale Romanum. Low Mass with one server is covered step by step; sung and solemn Mass at overview level.

## How to read this file

- **Rubric** means the 1962 books: the *Ritus servandus in celebratione Missae* and *Ordo Missae* printed in the 1962 Missal [1], and the Missal part of the 1960 Code of Rubrics printed in the same volume as *Rubricae generales Missalis Romani* [2]. "Rit. serv. VIII.6" means Ritus servandus, title VIII, paragraph 6. "n. 503" means number 503 of the 1960 Code [2].
- **1962 change** marks a point where a pre-1960 manual (chiefly Fortescue 1920 [8]) or the pre-1960 Missal [3] says something different from the 1962 books. The 1962 answer is the one to follow.
- **Current practice** means first-party guides from communities that celebrate this Mass now: the Canons Regular of St. John Cantius (sanctamissa.org) [11][12][13][14] and an FSSP parish, Immaculate Conception, Omaha [15]. No public ICKSP server manual could be found (see open questions).
- **Confidence** (on anything a server acts on): **High** = settled by rubric text read directly, or all sources read agree. **Medium** = from a manual or current guide, nothing contrary found. **Low** = single secondary source, sources conflict, or inference.
- **UNVERIFIED** = could not be traced to a source that was actually read. Nothing below is filled in from memory.
- Places in the sanctuary: the **footpace** (predella, suppedaneum) is the top platform where the priest stands; the **lowest step** (*infimus gradus*) is the bottom altar step; **in plano** means on the floor of the sanctuary below the steps; the **credence** is the side table on the Epistle side [2, n. 528]; the **sedilia** is the priests' bench.

## A. Basics a new server needs

### A1. Epistle side and Gospel side (High)

- **Facing the altar, the Epistle side is on your right and the Gospel side is on your left.** The rubrics name them from the altar's own point of view: after kissing the altar the priest "accedit ad latus eius sinistrum, id est Epistolae" (goes to its left side, that is, the Epistle side) [1, Rit. serv. IV.2]. Relics "a dexteris ... idest a parte Evangelii" are on the altar's right, the Gospel side [1, Rit. serv. IV.5]. At Communion the priest goes "ad eorum dexteram, hoc est, ad latus Epistolae" (to the communicants' right, the Epistle side) [1, Rit. serv. X.6].
- The Gospel side is treated as the side of greater dignity [8, p. 29].
- Older books say *cornu Epistolae / cornu Evangelii* ("horn", i.e. corner) [3, Rit. serv.]; the 1962 text says *latus* (side) [1]. Same places.

### A2. Genuflections (High unless noted)

- **Single genuflection:** stand upright facing the altar, hands joined; without bending the body, touch the floor with the right knee where the right foot was; rise at once [8, p. 21].
- **Double genuflection** (Fortescue calls it a "prostration"): genuflect, put the left knee down as well, bow head and shoulders slightly, rise [8, p. 21]. Used before the Blessed Sacrament **exposed** [8, pp. 21, 76].
- **When the server genuflects:** at the beginning and end of Mass and every time he passes the middle of the altar, **whether or not the Blessed Sacrament is reserved there** (S.R.C., 16 Nov. 1906) [8, p. 76]. The priest only bows profoundly when there is no tabernacle [1, Rit. serv. II.2, III.1], so at a side altar the server genuflects while the priest bows [12][15a].
- **In plano:** a genuflection made on the floor. Fortescue: "At the beginning and end of Mass the genuflection is made on the ground ... During Mass it is made on the lowest step of the altar" [8, p. 22]. Current guides have the server cross and genuflect on the floor at the centre during Mass too [11][12][15a]. (Medium; see Disagreements.)
- If you are about to kneel on the spot, do not genuflect first; if you are going to kneel on a step, genuflect on the floor first, then kneel on the step [8, p. 22]. (Medium)
- Current guides: no genuflection when going **up** to kneel beside the priest for the Consecration; genuflect at the centre when coming back down [12][15a]. (Medium)

### A3. Bows (High for kinds; Low for bowing while kneeling)

- Three kinds [8, p. 22]: **profound** (head and body, so the hands could touch the knees), **medium** (head and shoulders), **simple** (head only). The FSSP Omaha sheets use a "low head bow" = head with a slight inclination of the shoulders [15a, n. 2].
- **When the head is bowed** [8, p. 23]: the Holy Name; when the three Divine Persons are named together (the *Gloria Patri*); the name of Mary; the saint of the day; the Pope; the diocesan Ordinary when prayed for aloud; the verses so marked in the *Gloria* and *Creed*. "The person who says the word *Oremus* bows the head while saying it" [8, p. 23]; current guides also have the server bow at *Oremus* [11][12].
- **Kneeling or sitting:** bow straight ahead; turn towards the cross only when standing [8, p. 23 n. 2]; same in [11] ("does it straight forward"). Contra: FSSP Omaha's choir and two-server sheets say "when kneeling do not bow" [15b][15j], although its one-server sheet has the server bow at the *Gloria Patri* and from *Deus, tu conversus* [15a]. (Low: practice varies.)
- Nobody bows while making the sign of the cross; someone already bowing makes no further motion [8, p. 23].
- The server bows to the priest before and after handing him anything at the altar (cruets, Lavabo, ablutions) [8, pp. 79, 81][11][15a]. (High)

### A4. Hands (High)

- When the hands are not occupied: joined before the breast, palm to palm, fingers pointing up [8, pp. 23, 76].
- When one hand holds something, the other lies flat on the breast [8, p. 23][12].
- The rubric "pollice dextero super sinistrum posito in modum crucis" (right thumb crossed over the left) is written for the celebrant [1, Rit. serv. III.1]. Whether servers must do the same: UNVERIFIED.

### A5. Sign of the cross (High)

- Left hand flat on the breast; right hand touches forehead (*Patris*), breast (*Filii*), left shoulder (*Spiritus Sancti*), right shoulder (*Amen*) [8, p. 24]. The priest's rubric: palm turned towards himself, fingers joined and extended [1, Rit. serv. III.5].
- The server signs himself **whenever the priest signs himself**, not when the priest blesses something else [8, pp. 76, 78]. In practice: *In nomine Patris*; *Adiutorium nostrum*; *Indulgentiam*; the start of the Introit (not at Requiems); *Cum Sancto Spiritu* at the end of the *Gloria*; *Et vitam venturi saeculi* at the end of the *Creed*; *Benedictus*; the blessing; and the small triple cross (forehead, lips, breast, with the thumb) at the Gospel and Last Gospel [8, p. 78][11][12][15a].

### A6. Striking the breast (High)

- Three times at *mea culpa, mea culpa, mea maxima culpa* in his own Confiteor [8, p. 77][12].
- Three times with the priest at the *Agnus Dei* [8, p. 80][11][15a]; not at Requiems, where the priest does not strike his breast [1, Rit. serv. XIII.1][15a].
- Any other occasion (for example at the *Domine, non sum dignus* before his own Communion): UNVERIFIED, not in the sources read.

### A7. What the server wears (High)

- The rubric supposes a surplice: the server goes before the priest "superpelliceum induto" [1, Rit. serv. II.1]. Fortescue: cassock and surplice; the missal and the S.R.C. (23 Nov. 1906, n. 1) require the surplice whether the server is a cleric or not; he notes that in 1920 England servers often served in lay dress [8, p. 76 and n. 4].
- Current practice: cassock and surplice [15a][15b].

### A8. Where the server kneels: the general rule (High)

- **Normal place: kneeling on the lowest step, at the side opposite the missal** [8, p. 76][15a]. The rubric confirms it for the end of Mass: once the missal is back on the Epistle side, "minister genuflectit iuxta latus Evangelii, ut in principio Missae" (kneels by the Gospel side, as at the beginning) [1, Rit. serv. XI.1].
  - Introit to Gospel: missal on the Epistle side, server on the **Gospel** side.
  - After the Gospel to the ablutions: missal on the Gospel side, server on the **Epistle** side.
  - After the ablutions: missal on the Epistle side, server back on the **Gospel** side.
- **Prayers at the foot:** on the floor, behind the priest to his left: "Minister retro post eum ad sinistram genuflexus" [1, Rit. serv. III.6]; "on the ground, not on the step" [8, p. 77].
- **1962 change:** the pre-1960 general rubric "Circumstantes autem in Missis privatis semper genua flectunt, etiam Tempore Paschali, praeterquam dum legitur Evangelium" (those present at low Masses always kneel, even in Eastertide, except during the Gospel) [3, Rubr. gen. XVII.2] does not appear in the 1960 Code [2, nn. 517–524]. The server's kneeling now rests on the manuals and custom, which are unchanged (Medium).

## B. Low Mass with one server, step by step

Each step gives place, posture, action and words, with sources and confidence. A chart-ready timeline (B23), a list of positions for a sanctuary diagram (B24) and the two-server differences (B25) follow.

### B0. Before Mass

- Vest in cassock and surplice [8, p. 76][15a].
- **On the credence (Epistle side):** cruets of wine and water, dish and towel for the Lavabo, the small bell, and the paten for the Communion of the faithful: "parentur ampullae vini et aquae cum pelvicula et manutergio, necnon parva campanula, et patina pro fidelium Communione" [2, n. 528]. (High)
- **Candles:** two for Low Mass [8, p. 77][11][15a]. Light the Epistle side first, starting with the candle nearest the cross; extinguish in reverse order [8, pp. 77, 94 (S.R.C., 1 Feb. 1907)][15a]. (Medium)
- **Missal:** the rubric supposes the server carries it in before the priest and back out afterwards [1, Rit. serv. II.1, XII.6 "eo modo quo venerat"]; Fortescue notes it was commonly left on the altar instead [8, p. 77 n. 2]. Current: closed on its stand at the Epistle side before Mass [11]. **At Low Mass the server never opens the missal or turns its pages** (S.R.C. 3448 ad XIV) [8, p. 77 n. 1]. (High that the priest handles the pages)
- Help the priest vest [8, p. 77][15a]. Customs: offer holy water at the sacristy door [11][15a]; ring a bell at the sacristy door as Mass begins [8, p. 77][11][15a].

### B1. Entrance and the biretta (High, except where noted)

1. The server walks in front of the priest ("ministro ... praecedente") [1, Rit. serv. II.1], after bowing with him to the sacristy cross [8, p. 77].
2. At the foot of the altar the server stands at the priest's **right** (Epistle side) [8, p. 77][11].
3. The priest "caput detegit, biretum ministro porrigit" (uncovers and hands the biretta to the server) [1, Rit. serv. II.2]. The server takes it with the *solita oscula*: on taking anything from the priest, kiss first his hand, then the object [8, pp. 24, 77][11][15a]. (Medium: the kisses are a manual rule and current practice; omitted at Requiems and before the Blessed Sacrament exposed [8, p. 76].)
4. Genuflect on the floor as the priest genuflects (or bows, if there is no tabernacle) [8, p. 77][11][15a].
5. Put the biretta on the sedilia [11][15a] (Fortescue: credence or another convenient place [8, p. 77]). If he carried the missal, he puts it closed on its stand at the Epistle side, going round the footpace rather than across it [8, p. 77].
6. Return to the Gospel side of the centre and wait. When the priest comes down to the floor and makes his reverence, kneel **on the floor**, behind the priest to his left [1, Rit. serv. III.6][8, p. 77][11].

### B2. Prayers at the foot of the altar

**Place:** floor, left and behind the priest. **Posture:** kneeling. Texts in section C.

| Moment | What the server does | Sources | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| *In nomine Patris* | Signs himself with the priest | [8, p. 77][12] | High |
| Psalm 42 | Answers alternate verses; bows his head at *Gloria Patri* | [1, Rit. serv. III.6; Ordo][11][12][15a] | High |
| Priest's Confiteor | Kneels upright, does not bow | [8, p. 77][12] | Medium |
| *Misereatur tui* | Says it turned slightly towards the priest, bowing (slight [8, p. 77]; moderate [12]; profound [15a]) | as given | Medium |
| Server's Confiteor | Bows low towards the altar; turns a little to the priest at *et tibi, pater* and *et te, pater*: "dicunt tibi, pater, et te, pater, aliquantulum conversi ad celebrantem" [1, Rit. serv. III.9]; strikes the breast three times at *mea culpa* | [1][8, p. 77][12] | High |
| Priest's *Misereatur vestri* | Stays bowed, answers *Amen* | [8, p. 77][12] | High |
| *Indulgentiam* | Upright, signs himself, answers *Amen* | [8, p. 77][12] | High |
| *Deus, tu conversus* to *Oremus* | Bows slightly and stays bowed until the priest goes up | [8, p. 77] | Medium |
| Priest goes up | Rises; may lift the front of the alb; goes to the **Gospel-side lowest step** and kneels | [8, p. 77][11][12][15a] | High |

- **Omissions:** Psalm 42 (with its *Gloria Patri* and the repeated antiphon) is left out at Requiems and in Masses of the Time from Passion Sunday to Maundy Thursday inclusive: "Qui psalmus numquam praetermittitur, nisi in Missis defunctorum, et in Missis de Tempore a dominica I Passionis usque ad feriam V in Cena Domini inclusive" [1, Rit. serv. III.6][2, n. 425]. The whole prayers at the foot are omitted after certain rites (Candlemas procession, ashes, Palm Sunday procession, Easter Vigil, Rogation procession, some pontifical consecrations) [2, n. 424][1, Rit. serv. III.12]. (High)

### B3. Introit, Kyrie, Gloria, Collects

**Place:** Gospel-side lowest step. **Posture:** kneeling.

- Sign himself when the priest begins the Introit (not at Requiems, where the priest signs the book instead) [8, p. 78][1, Rit. serv. XIII.1][15a]. (High)
- **Kyrie:** alternate with the priest (who says the first, third, fifth, seventh and ninth) [1, Rit. serv. IV.2][13][20]. "Si minister, vel qui intersunt, celebranti non respondeant, ipse solus novies dicit" (if no one answers, the priest says all nine) [1, Rit. serv. IV.2]. (High)
- **Gloria:** said by the priest alone; the server bows his head at the marked verses (see A3) and signs himself at *Cum Sancto Spiritu* [11][15a]. Not said in violet Masses and Requiems [2, n. 432]. (High for not saying it; Low for the bows)
- *Dominus vobiscum*: *Et cum spiritu tuo*.
- **Collects:** answer *Amen* after the first and after the last. The Mass prayer has its own conclusion; any commemorations are joined under a second one, and there are never more than three prayers [2, nn. 435–437][8, p. 78][15b, n. 1]. (High)
- **Flectamus genua** (Ember days and similar): **1962 change.** The priest says both *Flectamus genua* and *Levate*; all kneel a moment in silence: "in ceteris Missis a celebrante proferenda sunt" [2, n. 440][1, Rit. serv. V.4]. Before 1960 the server answered *Levate* ("ministro respondente: Levate") [3, Rit. serv. V.4][8, p. 78]. The server is already kneeling and says nothing. (High)
- With several lessons, answer *Deo gratias* after each and stay in place until the last [8, p. 78]. (Medium)

### B4. Epistle and Gradual

- At the end of the Epistle answer **Deo gratias**. The priest usually gives a sign: he lays his left hand on the altar [11][15a], or turns towards the server or lifts his left hand [8, p. 78 n. 1]. (High)
- Then rise and go along the floor round the footpace, genuflecting at the centre, to the **Epistle corner**; stand on the floor a little behind the priest to his right during the Gradual, Alleluia, Tract or Sequence [8, p. 78][11][15a]. (High)
- With a long sequence (e.g. *Dies irae*) wait until it is nearly finished before moving [8, p. 82][15a]. (Medium)

### B5. Transfer of the missal to the Gospel side

- **Who and where (High):** "sacerdos, in Missis lectis, ipsemet, seu minister portat librum missalis ad alteram partem altaris in latere Evangelii ... et missale sic locat, ut posterior pars libri respiciat ipsum latus altaris, et non ad parietem" [1, Rit. serv. VI.1]. The priest or the server carries it. At the Gospel side it is set **at an angle**, the back of the book towards the Gospel corner, so that the pages face towards the middle [1][8, p. 78; "about 45°": 15a].
- **When (High):** when the priest finishes the Gradual (or Tract, Sequence) and goes to the middle for *Munda cor meum* [8, p. 78][11][15a].
- **Route (Medium):** up the side steps at the Epistle corner; take the missal with its stand [11]; come down to the floor at the centre; **genuflect on the floor at the centre**; go straight up to the Gospel side and set the book down [11][12][15a]. Small differences: sanctamissa bows to the cross before coming down [11]; the Biretta card bows to the priest [12]; FSSP Omaha does neither [15a].
- Then step down to the step below the footpace and stand facing the book [11][15a].

### B6. Gospel (and sermon)

- Standing by the book: *Dominus vobiscum*, answer *Et cum spiritu tuo*; *Sequentia sancti Evangelii...*, answer *Gloria tibi, Domine*; make the small crosses on forehead, lips and breast with the priest [8, p. 78][11][12][15a]. (High)
- Then go down, cross (genuflecting at the centre) and **stand at the Epistle side, on the floor, facing the priest**: "minister, stans in latere Epistolae post infimum gradum altaris, respondet: Laus tibi, Christe" [1, Rit. serv. VI.2][8, p. 78][11][15a]. Custom: wait for the Holy Name in the opening lines, bow, then leave the book [11][15a]. Genuflect if the priest genuflects during the Gospel [8, p. 78]. (High for the place; Medium for the Holy Name custom)
- At the end: **Laus tibi, Christe** [1, Rit. serv. VI.2]. At Requiems the priest does not kiss the book, so answer as soon as he finishes [15a].
- Then kneel on the **Epistle-side lowest step** [11][15a].
- **Sermon (Medium, current practice):** genuflect with the priest at the centre and sit at the sedilia or a stool; stand if the Gospel is read in the vernacular; genuflect with him again afterwards and return to kneel [11][15b]. The rubric only says a short homily may follow the Gospel, especially on Sundays and feasts [2, n. 474].

### B7. Creed (when it is said [2, nn. 475–476])

- Kneel at the Epistle side; this is "the undoubted rule" for the server (S.R.C. 2915 ad VI) [8, p. 78 and n. 2][11]. (Medium)
- Bow the head at *Deum*, *Iesum Christum*, *simul adoratur* [12]; **bow profoundly while the priest genuflects at *Et incarnatus est ... et homo factus est*** [8, p. 78][11][12]; sign himself at *Et vitam venturi saeculi* [11][15a]. (High for the profound bow)
- If there is no Creed, the Offertory follows at once; be kneeling and ready [15a].

### B8. Offertory: the cruets

1. After *Dominus vobiscum* (answer *Et cum spiritu tuo*) and *Oremus*, go to the credence [8, p. 78][15a]. Local customs: ring the bell once as the priest unveils the chalice [11][12]; go up and take and fold the chalice veil [15a] (Fortescue: "He may fold the chalice veil on the altar" [8, p. 78 n. 3]).
2. **Wine cruet in the right hand, water cruet in the left** [8, p. 79][11][12][15a]. (High)
3. Wait at the Epistle corner below the footpace ("at the foot of the predella" [11]; FSSP Omaha waits at the side of the steps and goes up to the top step as the priest turns [15a][15b]).
4. When the priest comes, bow. **Kiss the wine cruet and hand it over; do not kiss the priest's hand**: "accipit ampullam vini de manu ministri (qui osculatur ipsam ampullam, non autem manum celebrantis)" [1, Rit. serv. VII.4]. (High)
5. Shift the water cruet to the right hand (kissing it), take back the wine cruet in the left, hand over the water cruet, take it back; kiss each cruet as it comes back [8, p. 79][11][15a]. (Medium) The priest blesses the water and pours only "parum aquae" himself [1, Rit. serv. VII.4]; the server only hands the cruet.
6. Bow, return the cruets to the credence.
- **Requiem:** no kisses [8, pp. 24, 82][15a]; the rubric says so expressly for the ministers of a solemn Requiem: "Ministri, cum aliquid porrigunt celebranti, in hac Missa non osculantur eius manum, neque rem quae porrigitur" [1, Rit. serv. XIII.2]. (High)
- Historical note: Fortescue reports the Roman practice of spreading the towel on the altar and standing the dish and cruets on it [8, p. 79].

### B9. Lavabo

- Towel over the left forearm or wrist, dish in the left hand, water cruet in the right [8, p. 79][11][12][15a]. (High)
- Bow; pour a little water over the priest's fingertips into the dish: "ministro aquam fundente, lavat manus, idest, extremitates digitorum pollicis et indicis" [1, Rit. serv. VII.6]; offer the towel, receive it back, bow [8, p. 79][11]. (High)
- Put everything back and kneel at the **Epistle-side lowest step** [8, p. 79][11].

### B10. Orate fratres, Secret, Preface dialogue

- **Wait until the priest has turned back to the altar**, then, bowing, answer **Suscipiat**: "Et responso a ministro, vel a circumstantibus: Suscipiat ..." [1, Rit. serv. VII.7][8, p. 79][11]. (High)
- After the Secret(s): *Per omnia saecula saeculorum*, answer *Amen*; then the Preface dialogue (section C) [1, Rit. serv. VII.8][13].

### B11. Sanctus

- **Ring the bell while the priest says the Sanctus**: "Cum dicit: Sanctus ... prosequitur, ministro interim parvam campanulam pulsante" [1, Rit. serv. VII.8]. (High) Usually three rings, one at each *Sanctus* [8, p. 79][11][12][15a]. (Medium on the count)
- Bow (profoundly, per [11]); sign himself at *Benedictus* with the priest [11][12].
- **Sanctus candle, 1962 change:** the pre-1960 rubric had a torch lit at the elevation ("accenso prius intorticio") [3, Rit. serv. VIII.6], and Fortescue has the server light a third candle at the Sanctus and put it out after the ablutions [8, pp. 79, 81]. The 1962 Ritus servandus drops this; the Code only keeps the custom where it exists: "Usus accendendi cereum, prope altare, a Consecratione ad Communionem, ubi viget, servetur" [2, n. 530]. (High)

### B12. Canon up to the Consecration

- **Warning bell, 1962 change:** "Minister paulo ante Consecrationem campanulae signo fideles moneat" (shortly before the Consecration the server warns the faithful with the bell) [1, Rit. serv. VIII.6]. This sentence is not in the pre-1960 Missal [3, Rit. serv. VIII.6], and Fortescue wrote "There is no authority in the missal for ringing at the prayer *Hanc igitur*" [8, p. 80]. (High)
- **Moment (Medium):** the rubric does not fix it. Current practice is **one ring when the priest spreads his hands over the offerings at the *Hanc igitur*** [12][15a]; sanctamissa says "at the Epiclesis" [11]. Fortescue reports Van der Stappen allowing a ring at *Quam oblationem* [8, p. 80 n. 2].
- Then take the bell, go up **without genuflecting** and kneel on the edge of the footpace at the priest's right, a little behind him [8, p. 79][11][12][15a]. Fortescue's moment: when the priest makes the signs of the cross after the *Hanc igitur* (i.e. at *Quam oblationem*) [8, p. 79]. (High for the place)

### B13. Consecration and elevations

The rubric [1, Rit. serv. VIII.6]: "dum celebrans elevat hostiam, manu sinistra elevat fimbrias posteriores planetae, ne ipsum celebrantem impediat in elevatione bracchiorum; quod et facit in elevatione calicis; et manu dextera pulsat campanulam ter ad unamquamque elevationem, vel continuate quousque sacerdos deponat hostiam super corporale, et similiter postmodum ad elevationem calicis."

- **Left hand: lift the back hem of the chasuble** while the priest raises the Host, and again the chalice [1]. Only while he elevates, not while he genuflects [8, p. 79 n. 3]; not too high [11][15b]. (High)
- **Right hand: the bell** [1]. The rubric allows **three rings at each elevation, or continuous ringing** until the Host (then the chalice) is put down. (High) How the three are placed differs:
  - Fortescue: three separate rings; he may give one at the first genuflection, one at the elevation, one at the second genuflection, "altogether six times" for the two elevations [8, pp. 79–80 and n. 4].
  - Current practice: **1 ring at the first genuflection, 3 during the elevation, 1 at the second genuflection**, for the Host and again for the chalice (10 in all) [11][12][15a][15b]. (Medium)
- Bow (profoundly) during the priest's genuflections [11][12].
- After the chalice is put down and the priest has genuflected: rise with the bell, come down to the floor at the centre, genuflect, return to the **Epistle-side lowest step** and kneel [8, p. 80][11][12]. (High)

### B14. From the Consecration to the Agnus Dei

- *Per omnia saecula saeculorum* (end of the Canon): **Amen**. After the Pater noster: **Sed libera nos a malo** ("Responso a ministro: Sed libera nos a malo") [1, Rit. serv. X.1]. *Per omnia saecula saeculorum*: **Amen**. *Pax Domini sit semper vobiscum*: **Et cum spiritu tuo** ("Responso per ministrum") [1, Rit. serv. X.2]. (High)
- **Agnus Dei:** bow and strike the breast three times with the priest [8, p. 80][11][15a]; not at Requiems [1, Rit. serv. XIII.1][15a]. (High)
- Rare: if the pax is given with a pax-brede at Low Mass, the server kneels at the priest's right and holds it; *Pax tecum*, answer *Et cum spiritu tuo* [1, Rit. serv. X.3].

### B15. The priest's Communion

- Bow low while the priest communicates [8, p. 80]. (Medium)
- **Bell at the priest's *Domine, non sum dignus*:** not in the rubric; the S.R.C. tolerated it where customary (14 May 1856, as cited by Fortescue) [8, p. 80 and n. 2]. Current practice: one ring at each of the three, FSSP Omaha ringing at the word *dignus* [11][12][15a]. (Medium)

### B16. Communion of the server and of the faithful; the paten

- **1962 change, no Confiteor:** "Quoties sancta Communio infra Missam distribuitur, celebrans, sumpto sacratissimo Sanguine, omissis confessione et absolutione, dictis tamen Ecce Agnus Dei et ter Domine, non sum dignus, immediate ad distributionem sanctae Eucharistiae procedit" [2, n. 503]; the rewritten Rit. serv. X.6 has no Confiteor [1]. Before 1960 the server spread the cloth and said the Confiteor for the communicants, and the priest said *Misereatur* and *Indulgentiam* [3, Rit. serv. X.6][8, p. 80]. Many places still say it: see D and Disagreements. (High that the 1962 rubric omits it)
- **Bell, 1962 change:** "Si qui sunt communicandi in Missa, paulo antea ministrans campanulae signo eos moneat" (if any are to receive, shortly before, the server warns them with the bell) [1, Rit. serv. X.6]. The moment is not fixed. (High)
- **Server's Communion:** fetch the paten from the credence (current guides: after the priest's *Domine, non sum dignus* or once he has received the Precious Blood), genuflect at the centre and kneel on the edge of the footpace at the priest's right [11][12][15a]. Fortescue: on the lowest step at the Epistle side, sideways, or at the edge of the footpace [8, p. 80]. The server receives **first** (S.R.C. 1074) [9, n. 197][8, p. 80], and may receive inside the sanctuary at the edge of the footpace (S.R.C. 4271 ad 1) [9, n. 198]. The priest says the whole formula *Corpus Domini nostri Iesu Christi custodiat animam tuam in vitam aeternam. Amen.* [1, Rit. serv. X.6], so **the communicant does not answer Amen**. (High)
- *Ecce Agnus Dei* and the threefold *Domine, non sum dignus* are said by the priest [1, Rit. serv. X.6]. Current practice has the server say the *Domine, non sum dignus* with him [11], and some ring at each of these three as well [11][12]. (Local)
- **Paten:** the server stands at the priest's right and holds the paten under each communicant's chin [11][12][15a]. The priest starts at the Epistle end: "accedit ad eorum dexteram, hoc est, ad latus Epistolae" [1, Rit. serv. X.6][10, ch. IV A]. The 1962 rubric mentions the paten "sub mento communicantium apposita" without saying who holds it [1, Rit. serv. X.7]. (High that a paten is used; Medium that the server holds it; see D)
- Afterwards give the paten to the priest, who puts any fragments into the chalice [1, Rit. serv. X.7]; wait on the step below the footpace, then take the paten back to the credence [11]. Kneel whenever the tabernacle is open [8, p. 80].
- **Communion just before or after Mass, or outside it,** follows the Roman Ritual, not the Missal [2, n. 502]; in that rite the server does say the Confiteor, the priest then says *Misereatur vestri* and *Indulgentiam*, and the server answers *Amen* to each [10, ch. IV B]. (Medium)

### B17. Ablutions

- Take the cruets, **wine in the right hand, water in the left** [8, p. 81][11]. If no one but the priest communicates, go for them as he drinks the Precious Blood and begins to gather the fragments [8, pp. 80–81].
- **First ablution (wine only):** the priest, at the middle, holds the chalice out over the altar towards the Epistle side; pour wine until he raises the chalice or signals: "super altare porrigit calicem ministro in latere Epistolae, quo vinum fundente, se purificat" [1, Rit. serv. X.5][10, ch. II, ablutions][11][15a]. (High)
- **Second ablution (wine, then water, over his fingers) at the Epistle corner:** "deinde vino et aqua abluit pollices et indices super calicem" [1, Rit. serv. X.5]; pour a little wine, then water, until he signals [8, p. 81][10][11][12][15a]. (High)
- Bow before and after; **no kisses at the ablutions** [8, p. 81]. (Medium)

### B18. Missal back to the Epistle side

- "liber Missalis defertur per ministrum ad latus Epistolae, et collocatur ut in Introitu. Ipse autem minister genuflectit iuxta latus Evangelii, ut in principio Missae" [1, Rit. serv. XI.1]. (High)
- After returning the cruets: go to the Gospel side (genuflecting at the centre), take the missal, carry it to the Epistle side (genuflecting at the centre again), and set it **square** as it was at the Introit [8, p. 81][11][12][15a]. Then kneel at the **Gospel-side lowest step** [1][8, p. 81].
- **Chalice veil:** current guides then carry the veil to the Gospel side for the priest [11][12][15a]. Fortescue: "At Low Mass there is no serious authority for it ... It is better that the server, when he has moved the missal, go at once to his place" [8, p. 81 n. 1]. (Local custom)

### B19. Communion antiphon to the blessing

- *Dominus vobiscum*: *Et cum spiritu tuo*. Postcommunion(s): *Amen* after the first and the last [8, p. 78][15b]. On Lenten ferias the priest adds *Oremus. Humiliate capita vestra Deo* and the Prayer over the People: *Amen* [2, n. 506][1, Rit. serv. XI.2].
- *Ite, missa est*: **Deo gratias**. Variants (section E): *Benedicamus Domino*, *Deo gratias*; *Requiescant in pace*, *Amen*; in the Easter octave *Deo gratias, alleluia, alleluia* [2, n. 507]. (High)
- **Blessing:** the server, kneeling at the Gospel side, signs himself and answers *Amen*, then stands [8, p. 81][11][15a]. No blessing after *Benedicamus Domino* or *Requiescant in pace* [2, n. 508]. (High)

### B20. Last Gospel

- Stand at the Gospel side for the opening: *Dominus vobiscum*, *Et cum spiritu tuo*; *Initium sancti Evangelii secundum Ioannem*, *Gloria tibi, Domine*; triple small sign of the cross [8, p. 81][11][15a].
- Cross to the Epistle side (genuflecting at the centre) and stand facing the priest; **genuflect with him at *Et Verbum caro factum est***; answer **Deo gratias**: "quo finito, minister stans a parte Epistolae respondet: Deo gratias" [1, Rit. serv. XII.1][8, p. 81][11][15a]. (High)
- **1962:** the Last Gospel is always John 1:1–14, except the Masses of Palm Sunday that do not follow the blessing and procession of palms, which have a proper Last Gospel [2, n. 509]; it is omitted in the cases of n. 510 (section E). If omitted, the priest leaves after the blessing [1, Rit. serv. XII.5]. Before 1960 proper Last Gospels were frequent; the server then carried the open missal back to the Gospel side after *Ite, missa est* [8, p. 81]. In 1962 that can only happen on Palm Sunday (procedure assumed the same: Low confidence).

### B21. Prayers after Low Mass ("Leonine prayers")

- **Status (High that they are outside the Missal; Medium for the history, taken from [17]):** not part of the Missal (the 1962 *Ordo Missae* ends with the Last Gospel [1, Ordo p. 328]; "never been included in the Missal" [17, para. 12]); prescribed by decree of the S.R.C. for Low Masses (Pius IX 1859 for the Papal States; made universal by Leo XIII in 1884, recast in 1886 with the prayer to St Michael; the threefold invocation of the Sacred Heart added by Pius X in 1904; the intention of Russia given by Pius XI in 1930). The 1884 decree, as quoted in [17], orders them "recited, kneeling, at the end of each Mass without music" [17, paras. 5–7 and note 9; App. B].
- **When they may be omitted under the rules in force in 1962 (Medium):** J. B. O'Connell's list, as summarised in [17, note 14]: Low Mass celebrated with some solemnity (e.g. a nuptial Mass, or one preceded by the Asperges); Mass immediately followed by another function (e.g. Benediction); when a homily is preached; dialogue Mass on Sundays and feasts; when another Mass follows at once without the priest leaving the altar. Schmitz (1960): may be omitted on Sunday after a sermon or a dialogue Mass [10, ch. II "At the End", note 55].
- **Suppressed** from 7 March 1965 by *Inter Oecumenici* n. 48 j ("the Leonine Prayers are suppressed") [5]. Communities using the 1962 books continue them [11][14][15a] (see Disagreements).
- **Server:** the priest kneels on the lowest step or the edge of the footpace (S.R.C. 3637 ad 8) [10, ch. II note 55]. The server "kneels on the lowest step at the epistle side" [8, p. 82], that is, to the priest's right; sanctamissa has him meet the priest, stand at his right and kneel with him [11]. He hands the priest the prayer card if needed and takes it back [8, p. 82][11][15a]. Texts and responses in section C. They may be said in Latin or the vernacular [14][17, para. 8].

### B22. Recession and sacristy

- Fetch the biretta. The priest takes the chalice, comes down, bows (or genuflects if there is a tabernacle) [1, Rit. serv. XII.6]; the server genuflects with him on the floor and hands the biretta with the *solita oscula* (when handing: kiss the object, then the hand) [8, pp. 24, 82][11][15a]. Rubric: "accipit biretum a ministro, caput cooperit, ac praecedente eodem ministro, eo modo quo venerat, redit ad sacristiam" [1, Rit. serv. XII.6]. (High) Carry the missal out if it was carried in [8, p. 82].
- In the sacristy: bow with the priest to the cross, help him unvest [8, p. 82]. Customs: the priest blesses the server [8, p. 82 n. 2][11][15a]; FSSP Omaha has the priest say *Prosit* and the server answer *Pro omnibus et singulis*, then *Iube, domne, benedicere* kneeling, but no blessing after a Requiem [15a]. (Local)
- Afterwards extinguish the candles in reverse order and clear the credence [8, p. 82][15a].

### B25. What changes with two servers (Medium)

Sources: Fortescue [8, pp. 82–83] and FSSP Omaha's two-server sheet [15b]. The rubric itself speaks of one server at Low Mass and of "ministro, vel ministris" only in passing [1, Rit. serv. III.6–7].

- The **first server** does nearly everything described above; the second answers with him and bows and genuflects with him [8, p. 82]. Fortescue: the second kneels or stands "always on the other side" [8, p. 82]. FSSP Omaha: they enter side by side (first on the right), both kneel at the foot, then each keeps one corner of the lowest step, first server on the Epistle side, second on the Gospel side [15b].
- **Missal:** first server [8, p. 83][15b]. **Bells:** first server [15b].
- **Offertory:** first hands the wine, second the water [8, p. 82][15b]. **Lavabo:** first holds the towel, second the water cruet and dish [8, p. 82][15b].
- **Consecration:** both kneel on the footpace on either side of the priest and both lift the chasuble [8, p. 82][15b].
- **Communion:** at FSSP Omaha the second server holds the paten for the people [15b]. Fortescue (pre-1962): they may hold the cloth between them [8, p. 82].
- **Ablutions:** Fortescue: first server alone [8, p. 83]; FSSP Omaha: first pours the wine, second pours the water at the second ablution [15b].
- **After Communion:** first carries the missal, second carries the chalice veil to the Gospel side; they genuflect together at the centre, the first in front [8, pp. 82–83][15b].

## F. Missa cantata and Solemn High Mass (overview)

### F1. The roles

| Role | What the books say |

### F3. How a Missa cantata differs from a Solemn Mass

1. **No deacon or subdeacon.** The servers take over some of their practical tasks (MC at the incense and the missal, acolytes presenting the cruets directly) [8, pp. 136–142].
2. **Two forms** [8, pp. 136–137]: a simple one with one or two servers, done almost as at Low Mass (acolytes attend the celebrant at the seat); and a more solemn one with MC, thurifer, acolytes and torchbearers.
3. **Incense is optional** and since 1960 allowed at every sung Mass [2, n. 426]. **1962 change:** Fortescue (1920) calls incense at Mass without sacred ministers forbidden except by approved custom [8, p. 137].
4. The **celebrant sings** the Gospel and the *Ite, missa est* himself [1, Rit. serv. XI.3][8, p. 136]; the Epistle may be sung by a lector [2, n. 514].
5. **No kiss of peace**, no humeral veil, nobody holds the paten under a veil [8, p. 136 n. 1][1, Rit. serv. VII.9].
6. **Sitting:** at both, the celebrant (with his ministers) may sit during the Kyrie, Gloria, Sequence and Creed [2, n. 523]; servers may sit when the celebrant sits (S.R.C. 2515 ad V, per [8, p. 97]).

## Disagreements and open questions

### Where sources disagree

1. **How to ring at the elevations.** Rubric: three times at each elevation, or continuously until the Host (then the chalice) is put down [1, Rit. serv. VIII.6]. Fortescue: three separate rings, possibly one each at genuflection, elevation, genuflection, six in all [8, pp. 79–80]. Current guides: 1 + 3 + 1 per species, ten in all [11][12][15a][15b]. All are within the rubric's words if the 1-3-1 is read as "three during the elevation" plus extra rings at the genuflections; the extra rings themselves are custom.
2. **Warning bell before the Consecration.** Not authorised before 1960 [8, p. 80]; prescribed in 1962 without a fixed moment [1, Rit. serv. VIII.6]. Current: one ring at the *Hanc igitur* [12][15a] or "the Epiclesis" [11]; Van der Stappen preferred *Quam oblationem* [8, p. 80 n. 2].
3. **Bells at the *Domine, non sum dignus*.** Fortescue: no authority in the Missal, only tolerated [8, p. 80]; current guides ring three times at the priest's [11][12][15a]; sanctamissa and Biretta also ring at the people's [11][12], FSSP Omaha does not mention it [15a]. The 1962 rubric's own bell is "shortly before" the faithful's Communion, moment unspecified [1, Rit. serv. X.6].
4. **Bells at sung and solemn Mass.** Fortescue: unnecessary; never at High Mass in Rome; if used, only Sanctus and elevations [8, pp. 104–105, 142]. FSSP Omaha rings at the Sanctus, *Hanc igitur*, elevations and *Domine, non sum dignus* [15e][15i].
5. **The Confiteor before the faithful's Communion.** The 1962 rubric omits it [2, n. 503]. FSSP Omaha says it [15a][15b][15c]; sanctamissa gives both options [11]; the Biretta card calls it "permitted but not required in the 1962 Missal" [12]. Two private replies of the PCED are reported: 20 Nov. 2010 ("affirmative") [21b][21c] and 14 Nov. 2018, which in the published Polish translation reads, in English: "Certain specific conditions must be accepted: the liturgical books of the 'extraordinary form' no longer provide for saying (or singing) the Confiteor before the distribution of Holy Communion when this takes place during Mass. However, in those places where the practice exists, it may be continued" [18]. Neither original text was seen. Fr. Z (pointer only) urges following n. 503 [21d].
6. **Who holds the Communion paten.** 1929 law: the communicant himself (a priest or deacon at a bishop's or a solemn Mass) [4, n. 5]. 1962 rubric: silent [1, Rit. serv. X.7]. Current: the server [11][12][15a].
7. **Kissing the cruets.** The rubric names the wine cruet and excludes the hand [1, Rit. serv. VII.4]; Fortescue kisses both, before and after [8, p. 79]; current guides agree with Fortescue [11][15a]; the Biretta card is silent [12]; in 1920 English lay servers generally omitted the *solita oscula* [8, p. 76 n. 2].
8. **Bowing while kneeling** at the Holy Name or *Oremus*: bow straight ahead [8, p. 23 n. 2][11] versus do not bow [15b][15j] (and FSSP Omaha's own one-server sheet bows at *Gloria Patri* and *Deus, tu conversus* [15a]).
9. **Depth of the bow at the server's *Misereatur***: slight [8, p. 77], moderate [12], profound [15a].
10. **Where to genuflect when crossing during Mass:** Fortescue's general rule puts it on the lowest step, with genuflections *in plano* at the beginning and end only [8, p. 22]; current guides genuflect on the floor at the centre [11][12][15a].
11. **Chalice veil to the Gospel side** after the ablutions: done by current guides [11][12][15a]; "no serious authority ... better" not [8, p. 81 n. 1].
12. **Who handles the chalice veil at the Offertory:** server folds it [15a]; Fortescue "may" [8, p. 78 n. 3]; sanctamissa has the priest remove it while the server rings [11].
13. **Biretta's resting place:** sedilia [11][15a] or credence [8, p. 77].
14. **Server's place for the Leonine prayers:** lowest step at the Epistle side [8, p. 82]; sanctamissa says priest and server "kneel on the top step" together [11] (probably the step at the foot; wording unclear).
15. **Epistle at a Missa cantata:** "a lectore" [2, n. 514] versus "a ministrante" [1, Rit. serv. VI.8]; both allow someone other than the celebrant.
16. **Torchbearers at a Missa cantata and the "fast days" rule:** the rubric says torches stay until Communion "in diebus ieiuniorum" and at Requiems [1, Rit. serv. VIII.8]; Fortescue's list of which days that means is pre-1960 [8, p. 99]; FSSP Omaha simply keeps them through Communion because there are always communicants [15f].

### UNVERIFIED or open

1. Texts of the PCED replies of 20 Nov. 2010 and 14 Nov. 2018 on the Communion Confiteor (only second-hand reports and a Polish translation seen) [18][21b].
2. The October 1930 approval of the server holding the paten: reported by Wikipedia from Britt (1934); not found in AAS 22 (1930) or AAS 23 (1931); Britt not accessible [21a].
3. The S.R.C. decree number for the 14 May 1856 tolerance of the bell at *Domine, non sum dignus*: printed as "5224" in the scans of [8, p. 80 n. 2], which does not fit the *Decreta authentica* numbering for 1856.
4. The exact day of the motu proprio *Rubricarum instructum*: the scan of the 1962 Missal decree reads "23 iulii" 1960; it is often given as 25 July. Only "July 1960" is asserted here.
5. Whether the server strikes his breast at the people's *Domine, non sum dignus* or at *Nobis quoque peccatoribus*; no source read says so.
6. Whether the server signs himself when the priest signs himself inside the Canon (at *Supplices* and with the paten at *Da propitius pacem*): Fortescue's general rule would include it [8, p. 78], but no guide mentions it.
7. Whether servers cross the right thumb over the left when joining hands (the rubric speaks of the celebrant) [1, Rit. serv. III.1].
8. The ICKSP's own practice: no public first-party manual found (only a training notice, https://www.institute-christ-king.org/oakland-groups/1106-altar-servers-oakland).
9. An FSSP-wide manual: none public; the FSSP material used is one parish's sheets [15] and a chaplaincy page that points to the Canons Regular's materials [16].
10. Post-1962 law and the 1962 books: *Universae Ecclesiae* n. 28 says *Summorum Pontificum* "derogates from those provisions of law, connected with the sacred Rites, promulgated from 1962 onwards and incompatible with the rubrics of the liturgical books in effect in 1962" [6]. That may bear on whether the 1964 suppression of the Leonine prayers [5, n. 48 j] touches celebrations with the 1962 books, but no source read argues it either way. *Traditionis custodes* art. 8 (2021) abrogated earlier norms that do not conform to it [7]. How these interact is a canonical question not settled here.
11. Who may serve: Fortescue cites the 1917 Code, c. 813, on women answering from outside the sanctuary in necessity [8, p. 76 n. 4]; current law and practice were not researched.
12. Cassock colour, footwear, and other dress details beyond cassock and surplice: not covered by the sources read.
13. The exact procedure for a proper Last Gospel in 1962 (Palm Sunday Masses without the procession) is assumed to follow Fortescue's pre-1960 description [8, p. 81]; no 1962-era source read describes it.
